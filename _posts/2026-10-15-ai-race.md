A week ago, the race to build the most capable AI looked like a two-company contest between OpenAI and Anthropic. Then, within two days, Meta and Google both released models they say can go toe to toe with the leaders. As [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1011311/the-ai-race-is-back-on) put it, the race is back on.

## Monday: Meta arrives

Meta had spent billions rebuilding its AI operations, and its earlier models hadn't quite reached the frontier. On Monday it unveiled Muse Wonder. According to Meta's own testing, [reported by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/12/meta-releases-its-first-frontier-model-muse-wonder/), Wonder and Wonder Full score comparably to Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra on its benchmarks, and The Verge adds Claude Opus 5.5 to that list. Through Meta's API it costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, roughly in line with Opus 5.5.

## Tuesday: Google strikes back

Google had looked like it was drifting off the frontier, after a run of incremental releases and the departure of senior AI figures. On Tuesday it released [Gemini 4 Argon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/), which it says matches or beats the flagship models from OpenAI and Anthropic on many benchmarks. Google highlights a new state of the art of <mark>77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1</mark>, a test of long, real-world software engineering tasks.

## The twist: price

Tokens are the chunks of text an AI model reads and writes, and they're how these models are billed. Argon launches at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. The Verge reports that this undercuts rivals by as much as 80% per token during the introductory period. After that, Argon's price rises to $4 and $20, which The Verge says is still significantly cheaper than its rivals.

## Why it matters

The Verge argues that Google and Meta bring something the AI start-ups can't match: huge ecosystems of products used by billions of people. With open models also improving fast, it's getting harder for any one company to stay ahead on raw capability alone.

## The catch

The benchmark results come from the companies' own tests, and benchmarks capture only part of what makes a model useful. Prices and comparisons are as announced in October 2026 and can change quickly.
{: .catch}

With four companies at the frontier, the race shifts from who's smartest towards who's cheapest.
