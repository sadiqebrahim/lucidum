A new weekly injection helped people with obesity lose a quarter of their body weight on average, in a trial now published in the [New England Journal of Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2604169). The drug, retatrutide from Eli Lilly, produced <mark>some of the largest weight loss yet seen from a medicine</mark>.

## What the trial did

The phase 3 trial, called TRIUMPH-1, enrolled 2,339 adults with obesity who did not have diabetes. They were randomly assigned to a weekly injection of retatrutide at 4, 9 or 12 milligrams, or to a placebo, for 80 weeks. The dose was raised gradually, every four weeks, from a 2 mg start.

## How it works

Today's obesity shots copy gut hormones that tell the brain you've eaten. Semaglutide (sold as Wegovy and Ozempic) copies one, GLP-1. Tirzepatide (Zepbound and Mounjaro) copies two, GLP-1 and GIP. Retatrutide is the first to reach phase 3 that copies three: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon, [as Scientific American explains](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/blockbuster-retatrutide-trial-signals-a-new-high-water-mark-for-weight-loss-drugs/).

## The results

After 80 weeks, average weight loss was [17.6% on 4 mg, 23.7% on 9 mg and 25.0% on 12 mg, against 3.9% on placebo](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/phase-iii-retatrutide-study-demonstrates-30-weight-loss). For someone who started at 100 kilograms, a 25% loss means about 25 kilograms. Among participants with a starting BMI of 35 or more who stayed on the drug for two years, weight loss reached up to 30%. The trial also found less knee pain in people with knee osteoarthritis, and fewer breathing interruptions in people with sleep apnoea.

## The side effects

The side effects are the familiar ones for this class of drug, and more common at higher doses. On the top dose, 42% of participants had nausea, 32% diarrhoea, 26% constipation and 25% vomiting. About 11% of people on 12 mg stopped because of side effects, compared with about 5% on placebo.

## Why it matters

Obesity raises the risk of diabetes, heart disease and joint problems, and the drugs that treat it keep getting stronger. A three-hormone drug reaching an average of 25% raises the bar for what a medicine can do, as long as people can tolerate it.

Retatrutide is still investigational and isn't approved anywhere. The trial was funded by its maker, Eli Lilly. The two-year figure applies to a subgroup who stayed on the top dose, and nausea and other stomach side effects were common.
