The Big Bang may have left behind tiny black holes. Nobody has found one yet. A [new theoretical study](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-tiny-ancient-black-holes-dimensional.html), published in *Physical Review D*, asks what would happen to them if space had one more dimension than we can see. The answer: some would change shape, and some could still be around.

## Two "ifs" at once

The first idea is primordial black holes. Instead of forming when big stars die, these would have formed in the early universe. They are hypothetical, and some physicists think they could make up dark matter.

The second idea is the "dark dimension". Standard cosmology has three dimensions of space and one of time. The dark-dimension scenario adds a hidden extra dimension of space, roughly a micron wide, far thinner than a human hair. Ordinary matter and forces stay trapped in our familiar space, but gravity can extend into the extra dimension.

Because primordial black holes can be extremely small, a dimension that size could matter a lot to them.

## From 4D to 5D

A team led by Luis Anchordoqui of Lehman College in New York applied proposed rules of quantum gravity to work out how such black holes would form and how long they would survive. The question the authors posed was whether the black holes behave as ordinary four-dimensional objects or as five-dimensional ones.

The result is a mixed picture. Some primordial black holes would start out as ordinary four-dimensional objects, then <mark>become unstable and turn five-dimensional</mark>. Others, formed from cosmic strings (hypothetical thin defects left over from the early universe), would be five-dimensional from the start.

## Could they still be here?

The extra dimension also changes how long these objects last. The authors report that black holes formed from cosmic strings can have lifetimes comparable to the age of the universe. If so, some could still exist today.

## The catch

This is theory, built on two ideas that are both unconfirmed. Neither primordial black holes nor the dark dimension has been observed, and the work came from calculation, not measurement. The paper is peer reviewed, but that means the maths was checked, not that the universe works this way.
{: .catch}

If the dark dimension is real, ancient black holes could be hiding in plain sight.
