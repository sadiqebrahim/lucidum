Some things in physics happen so rarely that finding them takes decades. On 3 November 2026, the LHCb experiment at CERN [reported the first evidence](https://home.cern/lhcb-finds-evidence-of-one-in-ten-billion-particle-decay/) of a particle decay predicted to occur about once in every ten billion tries, ending what [UKRI calls](https://www.ukri.org/news/scientists-find-evidence-of-rarest-particle-decay-after-42-years/) a 42-year hunt.

## Beauty particles live a blink

Collisions at the Large Hadron Collider produce huge numbers of particles containing a beauty quark, a heavier relative of the up and down quarks in ordinary matter. They exist only fleetingly, decaying after around one and a half trillionths of a second.

## Two muons, once in ten billion

The decay in question is "Bd to mu mu": a neutral beauty meson turning into just two muons, heavier cousins of the electron. It is predicted to happen <mark>only about once in every ten billion</mark> beauty particles. A related decay of the Bs meson is about thirty times more common and was first observed by LHCb in 2015; the Bd version had stayed out of reach.

## Faster detector, sharper eyes

Since 2024, the upgraded LHCb detector has been able to record collisions at up to five times its previous rate. Combining new data from 2024 and 2025 with data from 2011 to 2018, and using improved particle identification and machine learning to separate the signal from the background, the team reached a statistical significance of 3.2 sigma. The 2024–2025 data alone gave 3.0 sigma.

![A sigma scale from 0 to 5 with marks for evidence at 3 and discovery at 5; the needle sits at 3.2.]({{ '/assets/posts/2026-11-09-rare-decay/sigma.png' | relative_url }})
*3.2 sigma: past the threshold for evidence, short of the five needed for a discovery.*

## Why it matters

Very rare decays are sensitive to new particles too heavy to be produced directly at the LHC: if such particles exist, they could nudge the rate up or down. So far, the measured rate is consistent with the Standard Model, and the analysis also gave the most precise measurements yet of the Bs decay.

## The catch

Three sigma counts as evidence, not a discovery, which particle physicists reserve for five sigma. This is a result presented at a CERN seminar, not yet a peer-reviewed paper. Earlier hints of this decay, in 2015, were not confirmed by later data.
{: .catch}

After 42 years, the rarest beauty decay has shown up right where theory said it would.
