Could you send a message to your past self? Science fiction says yes, and then has to deal with the paradoxes. A [new paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39735) gives a precise answer for one broad class of theories, and it isn't the one fiction promised.

## Loops in cause and effect

Some theoretical frameworks allow processes without a fixed causal order: A influences B, and B influences A. Loops like these allow what physicists call indefinite causal order, where there is no fixed answer to which event came first.

## The paradox problem

Loops are dangerous. Warn your past self about something, and you may prevent the very event that made you send the warning. A loop is only acceptable if it can't produce contradictions like this. The authors' test: a loop is consistent if it still gives valid probabilities however each party independently chooses to act.

## Causes may loop, messages can't

Hippolyte Dourdent, Antonio Acín and colleagues study deterministic classical processes. They prove that such a process is logically consistent <mark>if and only if it contains no signalling loops</mark>. A circle of causes is fine. A circle that would let information travel back to its own starting point is not.

![Two loops: a loop of causes marked fine, and a loop of messages crossed out.]({{ '/assets/posts/2026-10-01-time-loops/loops.png' | relative_url }})
*A loop of causes is allowed; a loop of messages is not.*

## The test behind it: Specker's principle

They also give a simple way to check a loop: list its possible events. The process is consistent exactly when that list is pairwise exclusive and complete. They describe it as the first characterisation of consistent loops that works from events alone, without imagining interventions or unrolling the loop step by step.

That condition turns out to be a multi-party form of Specker's principle, an idea from the foundations of quantum theory: if any two of several questions can be answered together, then all of them can.

The result also repairs an earlier intervention-free test that missed loops spanning the whole process.

## The catch

This is a preprint and hasn't been peer reviewed. It concerns deterministic classical processes, a mathematical model, not a claim that time loops exist in our universe. Quantum and probabilistic loops are not covered by this result.
{: .catch}

Nature may let causes go in circles, but it never lets you message your past.
